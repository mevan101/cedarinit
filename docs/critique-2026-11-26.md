# Cedar site critique, 26 November 2026

Audited: `claude/laughing-clarke-y3dcy8` @ `6b2e484` (the merged PR #7 redesign), built with `npm run build`, served from `out/`.
Method: Playwright 1.49 driving Chrome 141 at 1280×800, 768, 1024, 1440, 390×844, 360×780 and 320×720; axe-core 4.14 (`wcag2a`, `wcag2aa`, `wcag21aa`, `wcag22aa`, `best-practice`) on all 39 sitemap routes plus `/404.html` at 1280, 360 and 320; animations frozen and reveals forced before each axe run; `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`; 200 % zoom (640 CSS px at DPR 2); keyboard walk of 22 tab stops on five pages; image and link resolution for 155 images and 191 links; per-route transfer measurement over gzip; LCP/CLS from `PerformanceObserver`, plus a Slow-4G pass (150 ms RTT, 1.6 Mb/s down).

Two habits matter for reading this:

- **Every number below is measured on this commit.** Where a claim is a judgement (tone, information architecture), it says so.
- **Axe counts decorative text.** 56 of the 56 contrast nodes at desktop width are `aria-hidden` numerals or a watermark. The one real keyboard bug does not appear at desktop width at all. Do not read "56 violations" as "56 problems", and do not read "0 violations" as "accessible".

## Summary

The design is good and the build is clean. The site is honest about money and legal status in a way most nonprofits are not. Then three things undermine it:

1. **The homepage deliberately delays its own largest paint.** LCP is 3.2 s locally and 6.3 s on Slow 4G, because the opening sequence hides the hero for 2.55 s. With `prefers-reduced-motion` the same page paints in 0.9 s. Nothing about the network is at fault; the design is.
2. **Every visitor downloads a database client to render an announcement that is switched off.** 184 KB of the 714 KB of JavaScript on each page exists to read one row from Supabase at runtime, and on this sandbox that request fails on all 40 routes. The build already has the same data on disk.
3. **The one action the whole site exists to produce cannot be completed.** `/give/` has two buttons and both open an email. That is blocked on a payment account, which is not an engineering problem, but the page does not even answer "what does my money buy" any more — that content exists, on a branch nobody merged.

## (a) Defects that must be fixed

### A1. The opening sequence costs 5.4 seconds of Largest Contentful Paint

Page `/`. `components/Intro.tsx` adds `intro-on` to `<html>` and removes it after 2550 ms; `app/globals.css` hides `.orbit-group`, the header and the hero copy while `intro-on` is set (lines 537–562). LCP measured by `PerformanceObserver`:

- normal, 1280×800, localhost: **3,248 ms**, element `images/hero/group-banner.webp`
- normal, Slow 4G: **6,288 ms**, same element
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: **144 ms** local, **908 ms** Slow 4G
- 390×844 (phone): the LCP element becomes the intro wordmark at 1,260 ms, i.e. the thing that paints first is the logo animation, not the page

Google's "good" threshold is 2,500 ms. The difference between the reduced-motion and normal runs is entirely the intro: same bytes, same server. Also, `html.intro-on body { overflow: hidden }` locks scrolling for 2.55 s on the one page a first-time visitor lands on.

The animation itself is good work. It is 1.4 s too long, and it runs at full length on a 3G connection.

### A2. A database client is shipped to every visitor for an announcement that is disabled

`.next` chunk sizes on `/` at 1280: 19 files, 714 KB raw; the largest, 184 KB, is supabase-js. `components/AnnouncementBar.tsx` is mounted site-wide and, on mount, runs:

```ts
supabaseLazy().then((sb) => sb?.from('site_settings').select('value').eq('key', 'announcement').maybeSingle())
```

`content/live/settings.json` already holds `announcement.enabled: false` at build time, so the component returns `null` — after importing supabase-js and making a network call to `jczodfunfnbixlmdhgqr.supabase.co`. Consequences:

- one failed request and one console error on **40 of 40 audited routes** (`ERR_NAME_NOT_RESOLVED` here; on a blocked or rate-limited network, the same)
- a runtime dependency on a database for static pages that were exported precisely so they would not have one
- no visible failure mode if the fetch succeeds but returns junk

The build already snapshots settings (`scripts/pull-site-data.mjs` → `content/live/settings.json`). The live fetch buys "change the announcement without a deploy" for a bar that is currently off.

Second, related: that 184 KB chunk is also reachable from the header's account link. Next prefetches `/account/` because the link is in the viewport at 1280, and `/account/` is built on supabase-js. At 390 px the link is inside the closed menu, so the phone loads 353 KB of JS against the desktop's 714 KB. A nonprofit does not need to download an authentication client onto its homepage for a link most visitors never click; `prefetch={false}` on that one link fixes it.

### A3. The financial-report tables cannot be scrolled with a keyboard

`/financial-report-cedar-initiative-2025/`, `/financial-report-ushnota-abhijan-2025/`, `/financial-report-csedweek-2025/`. At 320 and 360 px the two tables per page sit in `div.overflow-x-auto` with no `tabindex` (`components/ReportView.tsx:125`, `:165`). axe rule `scrollable-region-focusable`, WCAG 2.1.1: 6 nodes across the three pages. A keyboard or switch user on a phone cannot reach the numbers, which is the whole point of those pages.

This never shows up at 1280 px, which is why two accessibility passes missed it.

### A4. Footer links are 19–20 px tall on every page

Every route, mobile widths. The footer is the only navigation to `/outreach/`, `/partner/`, `/transparency/`, `/fiscal-sponsors/`, `/our-team/`, `/brand/`, `/join/` and `/verify/`, and its 24 link rows measure 43×19 to 66×19 px. `.agents/skills/cedar-design-engineering/SKILL.md` states "Hit targets at least 44px". The rule is the project's own; the footer breaks it about 24 times per page.

For comparison, the header achieves 44 px on every item and the primary buttons are 48 px. The footer is the one surface that was never measured.

### A5. The accessibility work of PR #9 was reverted by the redesign, and nothing catches it

Today's tree: 56 contrast nodes at desktop width, all `aria-hidden` decoration — the "Force for good" watermark at `text-white/[0.06]` (1.17:1, 40 pages), the homepage `01/02/03` at `text-cedar-700/25` (1.52:1), the `/about-us/` value numerals at `text-cedar-200` on paper (1.25:1), the `/brand/` section numerals at `text-cedar-700/35` (1.83:1).

PR #9 removed exactly these three patterns and said why in a code comment: *"The mark, not a word: text at this size and this faint can never pass a contrast check."* The merge of #7 restored all of them without noticing.

This is worse than the numbers suggest, because of what it does to the tool. An axe run now returns 56 nodes that a reviewer must individually classify as "decoration, fine". The 57th will be a real bug and nobody will read it. Either the patterns go, or the verification procedure has to be explicit about what is accepted and why — and CI has to run it, because two people fixing this by hand twice is not a strategy.

## (b) Trust and conversion gaps

### B1. `/give/` cannot take a gift, and it stopped explaining what a gift buys

The page is one and a half screens. Two buttons, both "Contact us to give"; one `mailto:`; a "Where the money went" card listing three PDFs and totals. It does not say: what ৳1,000 does, when the fiscal sponsor will exist, what happens after you email, or whether a gift is deductible (it is not, and that is stated, which is right).

The missing section is not hypothetical. It exists on `agent/site-upgrade` (`/give/`, "What a gift pays for", built from the 2025 statement: programmes 61 %, running costs 28 %, launch 11 %, plus both programme reports' expense lines). It is stranded on a branch based on `main`.

The payment account is Evan's to open. The explanation of where money goes is not.

### B2. The sponsor strip overstates Cedar's backing

`/`, "Sponsored and supported by", `components/LogoWall.tsx` → `LogoMarquee`. It renders all 20 entries in `lib/sponsors.ts` in one unlabelled strip. Two of them (`Enhanfy Global`, `Youth for NextGen`) are Cedar sponsors. The other 18 — Google for Developers, Google Gemini, Gemma, Major League Hacking, DigitalOcean, O'Reilly, DataCamp, DEV — are ML Bangladesh programme partners. The data already carries `with: 'Cedar' | 'ML Bangladesh'`; the strip ignores it.

A visitor reads "Cedar is supported by Google". A journalist reads it and checks. This is the kind of sentence that costs an organisation its credibility in one email, and the fix is a caption.

### B3. There is no privacy notice anywhere

`grep -ri privacy app components lib content` returns nothing. The site collects names, emails, phone numbers, school names and districts through `/contact/` and the application forms, stores applications in Supabase, and issues certificates tied to identity. There is no statement of what is collected, why, how long it is kept, or how to have it deleted — not a page, not a footer link, not a line under either form.

For a youth-led organisation whose entire pitch is "we are honest about what we do", this is the loudest omission on the site.

### B4. The news module is a campaign diary

`/our-work/`, "From the programs": the six newest cards are CSEdWeek in Rangpur, Karail fire, and *Winter clothes drive, day 3*, *day 4*, *day 5*, *day 6*. Same school names, same photo framing, same three-line excerpt. A first-time visitor learns that Cedar logs its days. They do not learn what changed for anybody.

The content is genuine and should stay in `content/posts/`. The surface that promotes it should promote the four stories that have a beginning and an end.

### B5. Five of the six cities are not Cedar's work

`/`, "Rooted in Dhaka. Reaching further.": six cities, each with a one-line claim. Rajshahi, Mymensingh, Chattogram and Dinajpur are ML Bangladesh events, several of them before ML Bangladesh joined Cedar in September 2026. The heading says Cedar is rooted in Dhaka and reaching further; the body reads as six offices. PR #10 already fixes the sentence — this is the one place where it is ahead of the branch.

### B6. The homepage spends 1,680 px before the first number

`.orbit` is 1,680 px tall at 1280 (2.1 viewports), 2,539 px for `#work` on a phone. The first impact figure appears after two full screens of scrolling. That is a defensible editorial choice for a documentary brand, but it is the reason the hero has to carry the whole argument, and the hero does not mention money or outcomes.

## (c) Polish

- **Footer is 820 px tall on desktop** with the left half empty and the 6 %-white watermark across the bottom. It is the largest fixed block on every page.
- **Photo captions sit on unguarded photo backgrounds**: "Winter drive · Dhaka" is white text over a pale jacket in the hero mosaic, "Tech For Her · Chattogram" over a light ceiling. axe cannot evaluate text over images, so these will never appear in a report. Either a scrim or a chip, as `PostCard` already does with its category chip.
- **One currency symbol costs 47 KB.** `/` contains a single ৳ and therefore loads Noto Sans Bengali (47 KB) on every visit; the other three faces are 108 KB.
- **`outreach` page cover is 208 KB** for a card rendered at 372 px wide.
- **`/give/` H1** reads "Fund the next winter drive and classroom" — the article is missing, and in Anton caps it looks like a typo.
- **Sponsor marquee** clips its first logo at the left edge at rest, and the "Pause logos" control is 12 px text in the corner.

## What is actually good, and should not be touched

Said plainly because it is true, and because the fixes below must not undo it: no horizontal scroll at 320, 360, 768, 1024 or 1440; no overlapping or clipped headings at any width; 200 % zoom clean on four pages; CLS 0 on every page measured; 155 images and 191 internal links all resolve; the page is complete and correct with JavaScript disabled; reduced motion removes the opening, the parallax and the count-ups without breaking anything; 22 tab stops on five pages with a visible ring on all of them; the mobile menu is a native `<dialog>` that traps focus, closes on Escape and falls back to plain links without JS; the volunteer platform, the certificate verification and the report PDFs all work. The engineering underneath this site is better than most organisations ten times its size.
